Office food does not have to be an afterthought. A fresh cookie box is one of the easiest ways to make a meeting feel considered, thank a client without being stuffy, or give a team something they will actually finish.

Scratch Cookie Cottage bakes small-batch cookies from scratch in the Austin / West Lake Hills area. Orders are fulfilled on Friday and Saturday, with same-weekend cutoff Wednesday at 11:59 p.m. Central. That schedule works especially well for end-of-week gatherings, Monday planning snacks ordered ahead, and thoughtful gifts that arrive on a predictable weekend rhythm.

## When Cookies Work Better Than Another Gift Card

### Team meetings and Friday wrap-ups

A mixed six-pack—or a few of them—covers different tastes without forcing a group decision. Brown Butter Chocolate Chip anchors the box. Macadamia White Chocolate, Salted Caramel Chocolate Pecan, and White Miso Peanut Butter add range.

### Client thank-yous

A local cookie box feels personal and immediate. It says you paid attention, not that you clicked the first corporate gift catalog result.

### Onboarding and milestones

New hire day, project ship, quarterly win—cookies travel well across a small office and do not need plates, warming trays, or cleanup beyond napkins.

### Events and standing orders

For larger counts, recurring drops, or custom mixes, use the [wholesale inquiry page](/wholesale) so we can talk through date, quantity, flavors, and pickup or delivery needs.

## Practical Tips for Austin Offices

1. Order before Wednesday night for the coming weekend.
2. If the meeting is Monday or Tuesday, order for the prior Saturday so the box is ready to bring in.
3. Label flavors if you are setting out an open tray—especially White Miso Peanut Butter, which surprises people in a good way.
4. For peanut or nut concerns, have recipients check labels/ingredients and choose alternatives from the mix; do not treat any cookie as allergen-free unless confirmed.
5. Keep a wholesale inquiry ready if one team treat turns into a recurring ritual.
